### Findings

**1. Static props on approved surfaces are unmodeled — same trap class the plan already flagged for the bookshelf, missed elsewhere.**
- Severity: **Major**
- Affected Scope: §8.1 surface registry (D6), §8.2 rule 3, CC-003
- Simplicity Rationale: D6 makes every flat top placeable, but the approved surfaces contain drawn static obstacles the `SurfaceDef` schema cannot express: cutting board and kettle on the countertop (`assets.ts:320,322`), folded linens + basket on the linen shelf's upper board (`assets.ts:265-271`). The non-overlap rule (§8.2 #3) only checks placed items against placed items. A book dropped "on the kettle" is accepted, overlaps static art, and no rule objects — precisely the "feedback becomes nonsense" failure the plan itself names for the bookshelf decoys (§2, CC-010). The minimal fix is data-level (remove/move the props, shrink bounds, or add obstacle entries) and belongs in the plan + CC-003 before implementation, not as post-hoc bug tickets.

**2. The painter's-algorithm mitigation rests on a false premise; free placement makes cross-layer occlusion load-bearing.**
- Severity: **Major**
- Affected Scope: §8.6 ("handled by per-item depth keys that already exist"), §8.13 risk row 2, §8.9
- Simplicity Rationale: Items are a separate SVG layer appended *after* furniture (`scene.ts:219`), so items always paint above furniture; the per-item depth keys only order items among themselves (`gameView.ts:147-156`) — and they hardcode `x + y` rather than going through the Projector's `depth()`, contradicting §8.9's "one-line change behind the existing `depth()` abstraction". README.md:101-103 admits the limitation and notes today's level "keeps the scatter area clear of tall props" — a protection that seeded scatter + free placement revokes. Concrete case: a scatter point just north of the stool (≈ (5.6, 0.6): outside its footprint, ≥ 0.5 clearance, depth 6.2 < stool depth 6.85) screen-overlaps the stool and will draw on top of it. The plan should own the structural fix (merge props+items into one depth-sorted paint list) in CC-005/CC-010 acceptance instead of asserting the existing keys handle it; one merged sort is cheaper than per-case screenshot debugging.

**3. Slot/surface coexistence on shared boards is unspecified in the overlap rule.**
- Severity: **Major**
- Affected Scope: §8.2 rule 3 and restore/progress text, CC-004; interacts with §8.4 cleanliness predicate and CC-010 (16 slots *plus* tier surfaces on the same boards; same for sofa seat, entry mat)
- Simplicity Rationale: §8.4 counts occupants "on any of its surfaces **or slots**" — the plan itself treats slots and surface placements as distinct populations — yet the non-overlap rule never states that slot occupants participate in surface non-overlap, and no slot→tier mapping is defined (needed both for overlap and for "four books all sit on one of those tiers" when books arrive via slots vs. free surface drops). As written, a free drop on a tier can land on a slotted book and cleanliness can misjudge a mixed tier. One sentence in §8.2 plus a tier-membership rule in CC-010 closes it.

**4. (Directive b) Solvability verification is real but the solver's fidelity to the game rules is unspecified — a drifting second ruleset can certify unsolvable layouts.**
- Severity: **Major**
- Affected Scope: §8.7.4, §8.13 row 3, CC-013, CC-015
- Simplicity Rationale: The verification is not mere assertion: exported reachability validator + backtracking arrangement solver (all items placed, each color set on its own clean tier) run over ≥ 100 sampled seeds plus the shipped level, gated before epic close. Good. But the plan never requires the solver to enumerate placements through the model's own pure predicates (`validateSurfaceDrop`, slot rules, shelf-clean rule). A parallel simplified ruleset will drift and can declare completable layouts the game cannot finish — a soft-lock under the tidy-all win rule (D3/§8.4). Contract fix is one line ("solver moves are generated by the model's own validity functions") and is also the correct reuse answer: one ruleset, not two.

**5. (Directive a, YAGNI) `findSurfaceAt(x, y, carryZ)` has no consumer in the designed pipeline.**
- Severity: **Nit**
- Affected Scope: §8.2 API block, CC-004 interface contract
- Simplicity Rationale: The view resolves surface intent via `data-surface` stack-walking (`elementsFromPoint`, §8.3) and passes `DropTarget { surfaceId, x, y }` into the unified `drop()`; fit is `validateSurfaceDrop`'s job. `findSurfaceAt` is a second, model-side implementation of "which surface is under a point" that nothing calls — a duplicate targeting mechanism that can drift from the DOM resolution. Delete it from the contract or name a consumer before CC-004.

**6. Pinned geometry values contradict the art they claim to be pinned against.**
- Severity: **Nit**
- Affected Scope: §8.1 footprints/surface topZ, A6, CC-003 working values
- Simplicity Rationale: Bench seat top is 0.50 (`assets.ts:245`), not ≈ 0.40; the counter's placeable top is the countertop slab at 0.96 (`assets.ts:318`), not ≈ 0.88 (cabinet top, `assets.ts:310`); stool seat top is 0.49 (`assets.ts:338`), not ≈ 0.42; upright book dx is 0.20 (`assets.ts:407`, box spans x −0.1..0.1), not 0.10 (§8.1/A6). CC-003's own ±0.05 tolerance is already violated by its working values. CC-003 promises to re-pin against builders, but the plan presents these as builder-derived — correct them so the registry isn't anchored to wrong numbers.

**7. Two homes for the seed.**
- Severity: **Nit**
- Affected Scope: §8.1 `LevelDef.seed?` / CC-002 vs. A3 + CC-013 (seed on the game instance, `reset(seed?)`, `?seed=` param)
- Simplicity Rationale: `scatter(seed, level)` takes the seed explicitly and the instance owns the live seed; the level-data field has no consumer. Drop `seed` from `LevelDef` — one source of truth.

**8. Speculative field in the collection query.**
- Severity: **Nit**
- Affected Scope: CC-007 `collectionStatus()` → `tierSlotIds`
- Simplicity Rationale: HUD chips (CC-014) consume `complete`; the shelf animation (CC-008) needs at most `shelfId`. `tierSlotIds` has no named consumer — trim it.

**9. (Directive b, wording) R3's verification claim overpromises relative to the mechanism.**
- Severity: **Nit**
- Affected Scope: §3 R3 ("every generated layout must prove…") vs. §8.7.4 / §8.13 (test-time sampling)
- Simplicity Rationale: Runtime generation performs no check, and the 2³² seed space (user-suppliable via `?seed=`) is not covered by 100 samples; the real guarantee is structural (rejection sampling + fallback chain ending in the shipped, verified default layout). Also "reachability (free-space connectivity)" is loosely defined — drags fly over furniture (`gameView.ts:217-228` does pure position moves, no path collision), so connectivity reduces to "outside footprints + clickable", which scatter enforces by construction. Either run the cheap checks at generation time or restate R3 honestly ("sampled-seed verification + fallback chain"). Do *not* add a runtime solver — that would be the opposite mistake.

### Directive verdicts

**(a) No speculative machinery — PASS with two exceptions.** No engine, no physics engine (rules are plain AABB predicates in the pure model, §8.2 — the minimum set that expresses R1's examples), no rotation work (A6/§4; §8.9 analysis creates zero tickets), no hint implementation (§8.8 is a non-coupling guarantee only), ray-casting avoided in favor of the existing `data-*` hit-test pattern (`data-item-id`/`data-slot` already exist in `gameView.ts:318`, `scene.ts:183,195`; ghosts/labels are `pointer-events: none` in `style.css:66,80`, so the stack-walk is robust). Code search found no existing PRNG/scatter/AABB utilities to reuse (only a `clamp` local in `gameView.ts:37`), so CC-003/CC-013 are genuinely new, not reinvention. Exceptions: `findSurfaceAt` (finding 5) and the solver-ruleset duplication risk (finding 4).

**(b) Solvability — verified in substance, with fidelity and wording gaps.** The plan checks actual solvability (connectivity + backtracking arrangement solver incl. clean-collection constraint) over the shipped level and ≥ 100 sampled seeds before the epic closes (CC-015 gate) — not mere assertion. Gaps: the solver must be bound to the model's own placement predicates or its certificate is untrustworthy (finding 4, Major), and R3's "every generated layout must prove" overstates what test-time sampling provides (finding 9, Nit).

**No Blockers.** The plan is implementable as written once findings 1–4 are folded into the owning tickets (CC-003, CC-005/CC-010, CC-004/CC-010, CC-013/CC-015 respectively) as contract amendments before work starts.

## egm-inventive-catalyst (TRIZ)

**Verification pass (Step 0).** Every verifiable claim the plan makes about the current repo checked out against the authoritative files: baseline commit `39d9079` exists; 20 Vitest tests (14 model + 6 level); rejection union and restore semantics match `src/game/model.ts:9-14, 138-156, 164-212`; drop radius 0.95 / lift 0.55 match `src/view/gameView.ts:13-14`; depth key `x + y` matches `src/view/projection.ts:49`; the bookshelf decoy-book trap is real (`src/view/assets.ts:175-181` paints three static books plus a stack on the interactive z 0.68 tier); mute honored inside `play()` (`src/view/audio.ts:44`); `__cozy` hook is read-only (`gameView.ts:411-424`); Playwright is not in `package.json`; all 16 `dependsOn` edges in §8.10 match `docs/tickets/tickets.json` exactly and `scripts/tickets.cjs` verifies the DAG; 17 ticket records exist. The accepted constraints (no correctness hints, no camera rotation, physical-only rules) are user-mandated product bounds, not technical contradictions — the plan treats them correctly as immutable invariants, and §8.9's camera cost analysis plus §8.8's hint data-flow separation are honest. However, four false compromises survive in the foundational architecture.

### Finding 1 — Major
- **Affected Scope**: §8.6 ("Painter's-algorithm risk … handled by per-item depth keys that already exist"), §8.13 risk table row 2; subsystems `scene.ts` / `gameView.ts`.
- **False Compromise**: The plan presents the existing per-item depth keys as sufficient mitigation for 16 additional freely-placed items. The repo evidence contradicts this: depth keys sort *only within the items layer* (`gameView.ts:147-156`), while furniture is sorted separately inside the props layer (`scene.ts:175-178`), and the layer order is fixed — items always paint above all furniture regardless of depth (`scene.ts:219`: `svg.append(decals, props, ghosts, labels, items, drag)`). The README itself documents this as a known defect mitigated by level design: "an item dragged exactly behind a tall prop can momentarily overlap oddly; **the level keeps the scatter area clear of tall props**" (README.md:101-103). The upgrade deletes that mitigation three ways — free floor placement anywhere physically valid (R1), seeded scatter with only a 0.5-unit furniture clearance (§8.7 step 2), and items resting on furniture surfaces (D6) — yet claims the old mechanism "handles" the risk. Concrete failure site: a book free-dropped on a rebuilt bookshelf tier (depth up to x+y ≈ 3.0) will overpaint the plant standing in front of it (depth 3.5, props layer), and any floor drop behind wall furniture will overpaint it.
- **Proposed Resolution**: Unified depth-sorted render queue (Intermediary/Segmentation of renderables): one draw list owning both props and items, ordered by a shared depth key (+ existing `sortBias`), rebuilt on state change; ghosts/labels/drag remain intentionally-elevated overlays. The `depth()` abstraction in `projection.ts:22` is already the seam for this.
- **Secondary Liabilities**: Re-sort on every placement/drag state change (trivial at ~40 nodes, but it becomes a hot path during drags); layer-precedence decisions (ghosts, zone labels, drag layer) must be re-justified; `sortBias` semantics must survive the merge; e2e screenshots need re-baselining; the merged queue couples the scene builder to item rendering, increasing the surface area of `scene.ts`.

### Finding 2 — Major
- **Affected Scope**: §8.2 (`findSurfaceAt`) vs §8.3 (surface targeting) vs §8.8; subsystems `model.ts` / `gameView.ts` / CC-004 / CC-005.
- **False Compromise**: The plan installs two competing intent-resolvers for "which surface is under the pointer" without a precedence rule: the model's geometric `findSurfaceAt(x, y, carryZ)` ("topmost surface under the carry point", §8.2, listed in the preview pipeline in §8.8) and the view's DOM paint-order resolver (`document.elementsFromPoint` → `[data-surface]`, §8.3 step 1; CC-005 owns this text). §8.3's drop flow never calls `findSurfaceAt` — the two cannot both govern. This silently inverts the documented invariant "the view never invents its own rules" (`model.ts:39-45`, `gameView.ts:1-5`, README.md:66-69) and couples target identity to SVG paint order, which is itself an approximation propped up by `sortBias` — hence untestable in the node suite the plan brags about (CC-009/CC-015 are pure). Worse, precedence between slot aiming and surface targeting is unspecified exactly where they now systematically collide: all 16 bookshelf slots sit *on* tier surfaces, the shoe slots sit on the mat surface, cushion slots on the sofa-seat surface (§8.1 registry vs `house.level.ts:50-54, 72-76, 81-86`) — every drop near those anchors matches both resolvers. The plan's own risk mitigation "e2e drags assert the resolved surface" (§8.13) has no instrument: the read-only hook exposes no surface-resolution introspection (`gameView.ts:412-424`).
- **Proposed Resolution**: Single target-resolution contract (Intermediary): the view supplies the DOM hit only as an *elevation/disambiguation hint*; the model composes resolution deterministically (slot candidates via the existing carry-compensated `findDropTarget` first, hinted surface second, geometric validation always last) — or, alternatively, explicitly retire `findSurfaceAt` from the drop path and document paint-order as the authoritative resolver with a new read-only introspection getter on `__cozy` for e2e.
- **Secondary Liabilities**: One more contract to document and test; disagreement-handling rules between hint and geometry; the DOM-hint branch remains browser-only (node tests cannot cover it, so CC-009's "preview-isolation assertion" protects only half the pipeline); slight input-latency cost of always running slot resolution before surface acceptance.

### Finding 3 — Major
- **Affected Scope**: §8.2 rule 3 ("Non-overlap … with items already on that surface"; "Slots keep their exact current rules") and CC-004; subsystem `model.ts` occupancy ledger.
- **False Compromise**: The plan promises physical realism (R1: "wherever it physically fits"; §8.2: footprint non-overlap with ε = 0.01) but exempts half the occupant system from physics: slot drops keep "their exact current rules", and slot occupants never register a footprint on their hosting surface. Slot occupancy and surface occupancy are parallel, mutually blind ledgers, while §8.4's shelf-clean predicate unions them ("zero non-book occupants on any of its surfaces or slots"). Consequence: a shoe free-dropped on the entry-mat surface may legally interpenetrate a slot shoe at `mt1`/`mt2`; a cushion on the sofa-seat surface may overlap slotted cushions at `sf1`/`sf2` (0.45 × 0.45 footprints, anchors 0.5 apart); a free-dropped book on a tier (0.10 × 0.045–0.06 upright footprint, §8.1) may interpenetrate a slot book despite ≥ 0.30 slot spacing. Two books can occupy the same cubic space through entirely "valid" moves — violating the plan's own validity conjunction and making "clean shelf" feedback visually nonsensical in exactly the cases CC-010 exists to fix.
- **Proposed Resolution**: Unify occupancy into one ledger (Intermediary / single source of truth): a slot drop becomes an anchored surface placement that registers the item's footprint as a surface occupant; slots become sugar over `validateSurfaceDrop` at their anchor coordinates. Non-overlap then checks slot + surface occupants uniformly, and the shelf-clean predicate reads the same ledger.
- **Secondary Liabilities**: D4's claim that "existing 20 tests largely survive" weakens — slot/surface interplay cases must be added; restore-into-old-slot must now interact with surface occupancy (precedence needed if a free-dropped item sits in the old slot's space); slot capacity reasoning (A1's 4 slots/zone) becomes coupled to free surface area, complicating CC-013's solver (larger branching factor in the backtracking arrangement check).

### Finding 4 — Nit
- **Affected Scope**: §8.4 / A4 (transition-based feedback) vs the view's restore path; `gameView.ts:264-282`; CC-007/CC-008.
- **False Compromise**: The plan claims feedback is transition-based on every incomplete→complete transition of a committed drop, and that cancel-restore "re-drops into the old slot" (§8.2). But the view's restore path calls `game.drop(id, from.slotId)` and reads only `res.ok` (`gameView.ts:264-282`), discarding the result channel that CC-007 designates as "the single channel view feedback may consume" (`DropResult.collectionCompleted`). Cancel-restoring a book into its clean tier therefore re-completes its collection silently, while the visually identical manual re-drop chimes — an inconsistency in the "re-grouping celebrates again" guarantee. (Today's equivalent omission of `res.completed` is harmless only because `isComplete()` blocks pickup at completion, `model.ts:166`; the collection-transition asymmetry is new.)
- **Proposed Resolution**: Route the restore drop through the same commit-consumer as normal drops (single feedback channel in the view), honoring transitions identically on both paths.
- **Secondary Liabilities**: Slightly more view plumbing; restore animations and staggered chimes (A4's 250 ms) can now overlap settle animations, needing a small sequencing rule; no model changes.

### Net assessment

The plan's data contracts (§8.1), the shelf-clean predicate (§8.4), the no-hint isolation argument (§8.8), and the restore semantics under the single-held-item invariant are sound, and the ticket graph is a faithful, machine-verified DAG of the plan. But the plan accepts three architectural trade-offs as already-settled facts that the repo proves are not: cross-layer paint depth (Finding 1), single-ownership of target intent (Finding 2), and unified physical occupancy (Finding 3). All three sit exactly at the seam the upgrade widens — free placement over slot-bearing surfaces with 28 items — and all three have established, low-cost resolution patterns if addressed in CC-003/CC-004/CC-005/CC-010 contracts before implementation begins.
